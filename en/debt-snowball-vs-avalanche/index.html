Debt snowball vs. avalanche: which method should you use?

Both methods start with the same rule: pay the minimum on every debt and put every extra dollar toward one debt at a time. The difference is which debt goes first.

The debt snowball

You attack the debt with the smallest balance first. When it is paid off, the money you were paying on it rolls into the next smallest, and so on, like a snowball growing as it rolls.

Pro: you see debts disappear early, which helps you stick with the plan. Con: you may pay more interest overall if a small debt has a low rate.

The debt avalanche

You attack the debt with the highest interest rate first (usually credit cards). When it is gone, the payment rolls into the next highest rate.

Pro: it usually costs the least interest and finishes soonest. Con: if your highest-rate debt is large, it takes a while to see the first one paid off.

A worked example

Say you owe $4,000 on a credit card at 2% a month (24% APR), $9,000 on a car loan at 0.6% a month (7.2% APR) and $1,200 on a store card at 1.5% a month (18% APR), and you can put $1,200 a month toward all of it. The snowball pays off the store card first; the avalanche goes after the credit card first.

In the calculator, with these numbers both methods finish in 13 months, and the avalanche pays about $27 less in interest. The gap grows when the small debt has a low rate and the big one has a high rate, so it is worth running your own numbers.

Which one should you pick?

If your goal is to pay the least interest, the avalanche usually wins. If you have tried before and quit halfway, the snowball can work better because it shows results fast. The most important part is picking one and not stopping.

Before you start, try to lower your most expensive rates: a balance transfer or a lower rate from your lender can change the whole plan.
