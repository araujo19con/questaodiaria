How to make a monthly budget in 5 steps

A budget isn't about cutting everything. It's deciding ahead of time where each dollar goes. This guide works whether you've never made one or you tried and gave up.

Download the free budget template

1. Figure out your take-home pay

Add up only what actually lands in your account each month: your paycheck after taxes, retirement contributions and insurance, plus any side income. If your income varies, use the average of the last 3 months, or your lowest month, so you never plan with money that might not come.

2. List your fixed expenses

These repeat at about the same amount: rent or mortgage, utilities, internet, phone, insurance, loan payments, childcare and subscriptions. Pull the numbers from your bills and bank statements.

Add yearly costs divided by 12 (car registration, annual premiums, holidays, back-to-school). These "surprise" bills are the usual reason a budget breaks in certain months, so set the money aside every month.

3. Estimate your variable expenses

Groceries, gas, dining out, entertainment and shopping change every month. Go through 2 or 3 months of bank and credit card statements and take an average per category. Most people are surprised at this step: this is where the money that "disappears" shows up.

4. Decide what to save before you spend

Set an amount for your emergency fund, your goals or extra debt payments. A well-known starting point is the 50/30/20 rule: 50% for needs, 30% for wants and 20% for savings and debt. Try the 50/30/20 calculator to see the amounts for your income.

If the numbers don't work, trim wants first, then renegotiate fixed bills where you can (phone plan, internet, insurance, subscriptions).

5. Track planned vs. actual

A budget you don't track is just a wish list. Once a week, log your spending and compare it with what you planned for each category. At the end of the month, see what was left and adjust next month.

If you carry high-interest debt, make it a priority: the debt snowball vs. avalanche calculator compares both payoff methods with your numbers.
